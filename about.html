Our mission

Advances in genomics are changing how we diagnose and treat disease, but genetics specialists are scarce, and patients in rural and underserved communities often wait months or travel hours to see one.

SEGMENT connects clinicians to geneticists and genetic counselors through clinician-to-clinician eConsults, delivered by a network of experts across North Carolina, South Carolina, and Georgia who share informatics infrastructure. Along the way, we're studying what makes genomic medicine eConsults work for patients, providers, health systems, and payers.

Research

Learning what it takes for eConsults to last

SEGMENT is an implementation research study. Alongside the clinical service, we're gathering the evidence health systems need to adopt and sustain genomic medicine eConsults.

  1. 01

    Barriers and facilitators

    What helps and what hinders adoption of genomic medicine eConsults across different health systems and states.

  2. 02

    Patient and provider response

    How referring clinicians and their patients experience eConsults, and whether the recommendations change care.

  3. 03

    Value to health systems and payers

    The costs and benefits of eConsults from the point of view of administrators and payers.

  4. 04

    Sustainability

    The conditions needed for eConsults to keep running after the grant period ends.

  5. 05

    Scaling knowledge with AI

    Using the growing body of eConsults with AI tools to scale up and share expert genetics knowledge.

Informatics & AI

Knowledge that scales

Every eConsult answers a real clinical question. Shared data standards across partner sites will turn those answers into a structured knowledge base. We'll explore how AI tools can bring that knowledge to more clinicians, with genetics experts in the loop.

Funding

Part of NHGRI's Genomic Medicine eConsult program

SEGMENT is one of three regional networks funded by the National Human Genome Research Institute to study genomic medicine eConsults. The three networks meet regularly to compare what works in their regions.
